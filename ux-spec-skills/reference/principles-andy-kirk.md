# Kirk — Chart selection and workflow

**Source**: Andy Kirk, *Data Visualisation: A Handbook for Data Driven Design*.

---

## Core ideas

- **Purpose first**: Define the visualization's purpose along two axes—the intended tone (reading vs feeling) and the experience (explanatory vs exploratory)—before touching chart types.
- **Four-stage workflow**: (1) formulate the brief, (2) work with data, (3) establish the editorial thinking (angle, framing, focus), (4) develop the design solution. Skipping stages produces charts that answer the wrong question.
- **Match chart to the relationship**: Choose the chart family from the data relationship being shown—comparison, change over time, part-to-whole, ranking, distribution, correlation, or spatial—not from habit or novelty.
- **Five layers of design**: Data representation, interactivity, annotation, color, and composition are separate decisions, each made deliberately.
- **Trustworthy, accessible, elegant**: A good solution is all three; failing any one is a defect.

---

## For specs

- State the purpose (tone + explanatory/exploratory) and the data relationship before naming a chart type.
- Derive the chart family from the relationship (comparison, trend, part-to-whole, ranking, distribution, correlation, spatial); pair with `encoding-accuracy` to confirm the channel reads accurately.
- Specify the five design layers separately: representation, interactivity, annotation, color, composition.
- Require the editorial angle—what the chart is trying to say—so the design serves a point, not just the data.

---

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Purpose (tone + explanatory/exploratory) is stated before chart choice.
- [ ] Chart family is derived from the data relationship, not habit.
- [ ] The five design layers are each decided deliberately.
- [ ] An editorial angle (what it's trying to say) is stated.
- [ ] Solution is trustworthy, accessible, and elegant.
