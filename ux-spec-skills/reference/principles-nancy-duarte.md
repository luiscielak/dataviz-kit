# Duarte — Executive narrative and contrast

**Source**: Nancy Duarte, *Resonate* and *slide:ology*.

---

## Core ideas

- **What is vs what could be**: Persuasive narrative moves by contrast—repeatedly juxtaposing the current reality with a better future so the gap creates momentum toward the ask.
- **Audience as hero**: The presenter is the guide; the audience acts. Frame recommendations around what they can do, not what the presenter did.
- **Big idea**: One clear, stakes-bearing message per presentation, stated up front, that everything else supports.
- **STAR moment**: Something they'll always remember—a memorable stat, image, or turn that anchors the takeaway.
- **One idea per slide**: Slides are signposts, not documents. Each carries a single point with a clear action title.

---

## For specs

- State the "big idea" (one sentence, with stakes) at the top of any executive narrative or slide outline.
- Structure the arc as alternating what-is / what-could-be beats leading to a clear ask, not a flat status list.
- Give every slide or module a single point and an action title (a claim), not a topic label.
- Call out the intended STAR moment—the one figure or contrast the audience should remember.
- Pairs with `executive-slide-outline` for structure and Minto's pyramid for answer-first grouping; Duarte governs the emotional/contrast arc.

---

## Quick checklist

- [ ] A single "big idea" with stakes is stated up front.
- [ ] Narrative alternates what-is / what-could-be toward a clear ask.
- [ ] Every slide/module has one point and an action title (a claim).
- [ ] A deliberate STAR moment is identified.
- [ ] Recommendations are framed around the audience's next action.
