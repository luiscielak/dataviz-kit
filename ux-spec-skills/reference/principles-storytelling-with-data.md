# Storytelling with Data — Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic

**Source**: Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic, *Storytelling with Data*.

---

## Core ideas

- **Context**: Who is the audience? What do they need to know or do? What’s the takeaway? Without context, charts are just decoration.
- **Choose the right chart**: Match the chart to the message. Often a simple bar or line is better than a complex viz. Avoid pies for more than a few slices; avoid 3D and decoration.
- **Clutter is the enemy**: Remove non-essential elements (grid lines, redundant labels, legend when unnecessary). Aligns with Tufte’s data-ink and chartjunk.
- **Focus attention**: Use position, color, and annotation to direct the eye to what matters. One clear message per chart.
- **Tell a story**: Have a beginning (situation), middle (evidence), and end (conclusion and ask). Use narrative flow in decks and one-pagers.

---

## For specs

- Every dashboard and consulting output should state audience and “what they need to know or do” (purpose / decisions supported).
- Specs should call out the “one message” per module or chart; annotation plans (chart-annotation-plan skill) support focus and story.
- Remove clutter by design: specify only necessary grid, labels, and legend; avoid redundant encoding.
- Storytelling outputs (slide outline, one-pager): narrative arc (opening → evidence → conclusion → ask) and takeaways should be explicit.

---

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Audience and takeaway are stated.
- [ ] One clear message per chart or module.
- [ ] Clutter minimized (grid, labels, legend only as needed).
- [ ] Storytelling outputs have a clear narrative arc and takeaways.
